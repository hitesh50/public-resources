Logging, traceability and records for AI-Assisted activity

If it cannot be reconstructed, it cannot be supervised. This advisory sets out what Enterprise Risk Management expects of the evidential trail behind AI-assisted activity.

Section 1

Purpose

Every other control in this series depends on evidence. Human review cannot be tested without a record of the review; output quality cannot be trended without a record of the output; an agent's actions cannot be investigated without a record of what it did and why.

This advisory sets out the minimum logging, traceability, and records expectations where business processes are augmented with GenAI, including the interaction between AI logs and the firm's existing records and supervision obligations.

Section 2

The control objective

A control exists to achieve an objective. For this control, the objective is:

Control Objective

To ensure that AI-assisted activity can be reconstructed after the fact — what was asked, what the system produced, on what basis, who relied on it, and what followed — for as long as the firm is required to retain it, and in a form that can be produced on request.

Where a use-case cannot demonstrate what happened three months ago, the control environment around it cannot be tested, and the firm cannot answer a client, auditor, or regulator with confidence.

Section 3

Principles

Logs are a control, not exhaust

The log set is designed to answer specific questions the firm expects to be asked. It is not whatever the platform happens to emit by default.

Records obligations follow the content

Whether AI-assisted material is a business record or a communication depends on what it is and how it is used, not on the tool that produced it. Existing records and supervision requirements apply on the same basis.

Traceability runs both ways

From an output, the firm should be able to identify its inputs, sources, model version, and reviewer. From an input, it should be able to identify what was produced from it.

Logs are themselves sensitive

Prompt and output logs can contain confidential and personal data. They require classification, access control, retention limits, and inclusion in privacy and security assessments.

Retention and legal hold are design inputs

Retention periods, deletion, export, and legal hold must be established before deployment — particularly where the log resides with a supplier.

Section 5

Where standard logging is not sufficient

Enhanced evidential arrangements are required where any of the following apply:

Client-facing outputMaterial that reaches clients or forms part of a regulated communication.
Regulated activityUse-cases within investment, distribution, or other supervised processes.
Agentic actionSystems that act, where reconstructing intent and parameters is essential to investigation.
Supplier-held logsThe firm cannot access, export, or place a hold on the log without supplier cooperation.
ERM expectation: in these cases, evidential requirements are agreed with Compliance, Legal, and Records Management before deployment, and export and hold capability is confirmed contractually and tested — not assumed to exist because the supplier displays a history in its interface.
Section 6

Roles & responsibilities

First line

Use-case owners

Specify and implement the log set, confirm retention with Records Management, and test reconstruction.

Governance

AI governance

Confirm at intake that the evidential design supports the controls being claimed.

Second line

Enterprise risk management

Test reconstructability on a risk basis and challenge use-cases where evidence cannot be produced.

Third line

Internal audit

Provide independent assurance over evidential completeness for selected use-cases.
