AI use-case intake and risk tiering — expectations and minimum standards

The firm cannot control what it cannot see. This advisory sets out what Enterprise Risk Management expects before a GenAI or agentic capability is introduced into a business process.

Section 1

Purpose

GenAI capability now enters the firm through many doors: enterprise assistants, features switched on inside software the firm already licenses, developer tooling, and agent frameworks assembled by teams themselves. Much of it requires no procurement event and no project.

Where capability is introduced without registration, the firm carries risk it has not assessed and cannot report on. This advisory sets out what ERM expects of intake, registration, and risk tiering so that oversight is proportionate rather than uniform, and so that the AI inventory is a reliable basis for governance reporting.

Section 2

The control objective

A control exists to achieve an objective. For this control, the objective is:

Control Objective

To ensure that every AI use-case which influences a business process, decision, or communication is identified, recorded against the firm's risk taxonomy, assessed proportionately to its risk, and approved by an accountable owner before it is relied upon.

Registration is not an approval, and approval of a pilot is not approval of production use. A use-case that cannot be described in terms of the process it affects and the consequence of it being wrong has not been defined well enough to be assessed.

Section 3

Principles

Registration is triggered by use, not by procurement

A capability that costs nothing, sits inside an existing licence, or was built in an afternoon is in scope if it touches firm data or informs firm decisions. The absence of a purchase order is not the absence of risk.

Tiering reflects consequence and autonomy

Risk tier is driven by what depends on the output, how reversible the effect is, how many clients or employees are affected, and how much the system is permitted to do without a human. Technical novelty is not a tiering criterion.

One inventory, one taxonomy

Use-cases are recorded in a single inventory and mapped to the firm's existing risk taxonomy. AI risk is not a separate category of risk; it is a new driver of familiar risks.

Material change re-triggers intake

Extending a use-case to new data, new users, new jurisdictions, or new autonomy is a new assessment. Capability that grows quietly is the most common source of unassessed exposure.

Accountability sits with the business

A named business owner is accountable for the use-case — not the technology function that enabled it, and not the vendor that supplies it.

Section 5

Where intake alone is not sufficient

Intake is a gating control. It cannot be relied upon as the primary assurance where any of the following apply:

Shadow adoptionCapability is available to staff without passing through any intake trigger.
Default-on vendor featuresAI functionality is enabled by a supplier inside software already in use.
Pilot creepA time-boxed pilot becomes business-as-usual without re-assessment.
Evolving capabilityAgentic use-cases where tools, data access, or autonomy change after registration.
ERM expectation: intake must be supported by detective controls — discovery of AI traffic and tooling, supplier change notification, periodic attestation, and a low-friction route for staff to register use-cases without fear of penalty. An intake process that is easier to bypass than to complete will be bypassed.
Section 6

Roles & responsibilities

First line

Use-case owners

Register use-cases before use, keep inventory entries accurate, and re-attest at the defined frequency.

Governance

AI governance

Operate intake and tiering, challenge under-tiering, and maintain the inventory and criteria.

Second line

Enterprise risk management

Provide independent challenge, test control design and operation on a risk basis, and maintain this advisory.

Third line

Internal audit

Provide independent assurance over the framework and selected use-cases.
