Data entering AI systems — confidentiality and input controls

A prompt is an outbound data transfer. This advisory sets out what Enterprise Risk Management expects of controls over what may be submitted to an AI system, and under what terms.

Section 1

Purpose

The most common AI incident in a firm like ours is not an exotic model failure. It is confidential, personal, or client-identifying information being placed into a system that was never approved to receive it, retained for longer than intended, or processed in a jurisdiction that was never assessed.

This advisory sets out the minimum input controls ERM expects where business processes are augmented with GenAI, including retrieval-based assistants and agents that read from firm systems.

Section 2

The control objective

A control exists to achieve an objective. For this control, the objective is:

Control Objective

To ensure that information is submitted to an AI system only where that system is approved to receive data of that classification, that the volume and sensitivity of data submitted is the minimum necessary, and that the firm can establish afterwards what was sent, where it went, and how long it persisted.

Where a use-case cannot state which classifications of data it handles, the input control has not been designed. “Staff are told not to paste confidential information” is an instruction, not a control.

Section 3

Principles

Approval is per tool and per data class

A tool approved for internal, non-personal content is not thereby approved for client data or personal data. The approval record must state which classifications are permitted.

Minimization is a control, not a courtesy

The design should submit the least data capable of achieving the outcome — extracts rather than whole records, references rather than identifiers, and redaction or tokenization where feasible.

Retention and training terms are part of the control

Whether the provider retains inputs, uses them to improve models, or shares them with sub-processors determines what may be submitted. These terms must be confirmed and evidenced, not assumed from marketing material.

Retrieval inherits entitlements

Where an assistant retrieves from firm repositories, it must respect the entitlements of the requesting user. Retrieval that reads with elevated rights turns an access control into a disclosure channel.

Aggregation changes classification

Individually permitted items can combine into an output that is more sensitive than either input. Assess the data the use-case produces, not only the data it consumes.

Section 5

Where standard input controls are not sufficient

Additional controls and specialist review are required where any of the following apply:

Personal or special-category dataPrivacy assessment and a lawful basis are required before submission.
Material non-public informationDeal, issuer, or inside information requires Compliance review and access restriction.
Client-identifying dataClient confidentiality obligations and mandate terms may prohibit disclosure to third-party processors.
Unmanaged tools or devicesConsumer-grade services and personal devices sit outside the firm's technical enforcement perimeter.
ERM expectation: in these cases, submission is permitted only under an assessed and documented arrangement with Privacy, Compliance, and Information Security involvement — typically an isolated tenancy with training disabled, contractual deletion rights, and enforced egress controls.
Section 6

Roles & responsibilities

First line

Use-case owners

Determine and record the data classifications involved, apply minimization, and use only approved tools.

Governance

AI governance

Maintain the approved tool list and classification mapping, and challenge submissions that understate data sensitivity.

Second line

Enterprise risk management

Provide independent challenge, test control design and operation on a risk basis, and maintain this advisory.

Third line

Internal audit

Provide independent assurance over the framework and selected use-cases.
