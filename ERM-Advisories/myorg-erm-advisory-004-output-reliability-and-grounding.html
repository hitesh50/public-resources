Output reliability — grounding, validation and hallucination controls

Fluency is not accuracy. This advisory sets out what Enterprise Risk Management expects of controls over the correctness and verifiability of AI-generated output.

Section 1

Purpose

GenAI systems produce plausible, well-formed output regardless of whether the underlying content is correct. The failure mode that matters in an investment management firm is not obviously wrong output; it is output that is subtly wrong and confidently expressed, in a context where the reader has no independent basis to detect it.

This advisory sets out the minimum standards ERM expects for output reliability where GenAI is integrated into a business process, and complements the expectations for human review set out in ERM-ADV-AI-001.

Section 2

The control objective

A control exists to achieve an objective. For this control, the objective is:

Control Objective

To ensure that AI-generated output relied upon in a business process is accurate to a defined standard, attributable to a verifiable source where the use-case depends on factual content, and identifiable as unreliable before it informs an action, decision, or communication.

A use-case that cannot state what “correct” means for its output cannot demonstrate that its output is correct. The accuracy standard is part of the control design, not something to be settled after deployment.

Section 3

Principles

Fluency is not accuracy

Presentation quality carries no information about factual correctness. Users, and control designs, must not treat a well-structured answer as a validated one.

Grounding beats instruction

Retrieval from governed, current sources with source attribution is materially more reliable than instructing a model not to invent content. Where factual accuracy matters, ground the output and cite the source.

Verifiability is a design requirement

If the reviewer cannot check the output against something, the review cannot be effective. Source links, extracts, and calculation trails should be produced alongside the answer, not on request.

Self-reported confidence is not evidence

A model's expressed certainty, and its willingness to provide a citation, are not indicators of correctness. Citations must resolve to sources that exist and that support the claim.

Validate at the point of consequence

Controls should sit where the output takes effect — before it reaches a client, a filing, a system of record, or an executed action — rather than at an earlier, more convenient point in the workflow.

Section 5

Where human review cannot be relied on to catch errors

Output validation must be automated or structurally reinforced — not left to reviewer judgement — where any of the following apply:

Quantitative outputFigures, calculations, and data-lineage errors that are not detectable by reading.
Regulated communicationsClient-facing, marketing, or filed material subject to review and record-keeping obligations.
Interpretive contentLegal, tax, regulatory, or investment interpretation presented as settled.
Low base-rate errorsErrors rare enough that reviewers stop expecting them, but consequential when they occur.
ERM expectation: in these cases, reliance on reviewer inspection alone is not acceptable. Complementary controls are required — reconciliation against an authoritative source, deterministic calculation outside the model, mandatory citation resolution, or existing review and approval routes applied to the AI-assisted output on the same basis as any other.
Section 6

Roles & responsibilities

First line

Use-case owners

Define the accuracy standard, evaluate before deployment, operate sampling, and act on observed error trends.

Governance

AI governance

Challenge accuracy standards that are absent or unmeasurable, and confirm evaluation evidence at intake.

Second line

Enterprise risk management

Provide independent challenge, test control design and operation on a risk basis, and maintain this advisory.

Third line

Internal audit

Provide independent assurance over the framework and selected use-cases.
